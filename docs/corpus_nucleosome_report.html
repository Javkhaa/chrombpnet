01 · The ceiling

The model reaches 47% of what the data allows

The dyad target is real signal, with a clear 200 bp periodicity. Its split-half ceiling is 0.77, the best profile r that any model can reach on this data. The 346-cell model reaches 0.369, which is 47% of the ceiling. The accessibility head of the same model reaches 66% of its own ceiling. The trunk can learn structure at this scale, so the nucleosome head is the weak part.

02 · Cell-type signal

About one third of positioning differs by cell type

We measured how well nucleosome profiles agree between cell types, with a correction for sequencing depth. If two cell types had identical positioning, their cross-correlation would equal √(ceiling × ceiling). For well-measured pairs, it is 0.68 of that value. About one third of reproducible positioning is specific to the cell type, and about two thirds is a shared backbone. There is real contrast for a model to learn.

03 · Where it occurs

841 candidate sites in a mixed 20-cell panel

The differential-nucleosome caller compares each cell with the consensus of the other cells. It removes occupancy, so it tests position, not amount. It calls a site when the cell is a robust-z outlier and the difference is larger than that cell's split-half noise. The first panel had 20 cells: fetal neuron, cardiac, retina, hepatic, renal, PBMC B and plasma cells, GM12878 and MCF7.

Result

  • 841 calls (5.4% of tested windows), median 30 per cell
  • Median shift about 30 bp; 90th percentile about 78 bp
  • Example: a GM12878 site shifted −54 bp at z = 9.4

Limits

  • The panel includes fetal cells and two cell lines, so these calls are candidates, not an adult atlas. Section 10 re-scores them in adult cells.
  • The 5% of strong outliers agree with the broad one-third contrast: most windows differ a little, and few differ sharply.
04 · The metric

Score the deviation, not the shared backbone

Absolute profile r rewards a model that predicts the shared backbone well, even if it predicts no cell-type difference. We scored the deviation instead: how each cell's profile differs from the consensus of the other cells. Sections 04 to 09 use these two metrics on the 20-cell panel:

deviation r
Pearson r between predicted and observed deviation, per window and cell, averaged over all comparable windows.
shift r
Pearson r between the predicted and observed dyad shift, across the called sites.
FiLM baseline, 346 cellsvalue
deviation r, all comparable windows0.030
deviation r, called sites only0.217
shift r0.067 ≈ 0

The baseline detects that a called site is different, but it does not predict the direction of the shift. Its profile r of 0.37 comes almost fully from the backbone.

05 · The fix

FiLM cannot shift a profile; a per-cell head can

FiLM scales and offsets each feature channel. That changes how strongly features contribute, but it cannot move the output profile along the sequence. A per-cell 75 bp convolution head can move the dyad. We also started from the cfDNA positioning trunk, which was validated before.

change (20-cell panel)deviation rshift r
FiLM baseline0.0300.067

The per-cell head moves shift r from about 0 to 0.17 and gives about 3× the deviation r. More cells did not help FiLM. The head was the bottleneck, not the data or the initialization.

06 · Matched comparison

Each architecture wins one metric

We trained the per-cell head and FiLM on the same 60 cells, with the same budget and the same cfDNA initialization:

60 cells, matchednuc profile rdeviation rshift r
FiLM (shared head)0.4350.0270.065
per-cell head0.4010.1750.202

FiLM fits the shared backbone better, so its absolute profile r is higher. The per-cell head predicts real per-cell shifts, with 6.5× the deviation r and 3× the shift r. It got this result with less training (it stopped early at 11 min, against 35 min for FiLM). The next step was to put both parts into one model.

Caveats

Both arms trained for about 1 epoch, and FiLM got about 2× the steps before it stopped. This helps its absolute number. An earlier claim of "0.53 / 68% of ceiling" came from an occupancy-removed pipeline and is not comparable with these values. The reliable finding is the split between the two metrics.

07 · The hybrid

The hybrid keeps both strengths

The hybrid has a FiLM-conditioned trunk and a shared accessibility head, which are the parts where FiLM wins. It adds a per-cell nucleosome head, which is the part that can move a dyad. We compared the three models at exactly 9,000 steps, with early stopping off, so no model got more training:

The hybrid ties FiLM on absolute profile r (0.439 against 0.437). An earlier run showed a hybrid win on this metric, but that run gave the hybrid 19k steps against 9k. The real gain is the cell-type signal: 31× the deviation r and about 10× the shift r of FiLM. FiLM cannot predict a spatial shift at any number of steps.

08 · Longer training

Longer training trades the cell-type signal for the backbone

We continued the 45k hybrid with a larger budget. It stopped early at 77.5k steps. The absolute metrics improved, but the cell-type signal collapsed:

346 cellsnuc profile racc AUROCdeviation rshift r
FiLM baseline0.3690.6860.0300.067
hybrid, 45k steps0.3750.6570.1840.166
hybrid, 77.5k steps0.3890.6800.0480.155

Longer training added 0.014 to nucleosome profile r and restored accessibility. Deviation r fell from 0.184 to 0.048, close to the FiLM baseline. The extra training went into the shared backbone.

The predicted failure, measured

The design doc predicted this failure ("Role-b"): a conditioned model learns the shared component first, so it must be judged on deviation. Here, absolute metrics and deviation r move in opposite directions as training continues. A checkpoint chosen on validation loss loses the cell-type signal. We keep both checkpoints: 45k for cell-type work, and 77.5k for the absolute profile.

09 · Training on a probe

The probe score went up, and the eval went down

Next, we tried to train for deviation directly. We built a probe: a fixed set of windows, scored across many cell types at the same time. The probe score is the deviation r on these windows. We used it in two ways:

Run 1 · selection

  • Choose the best checkpoint and stop early on the probe score, not on validation loss
  • Flag --select-on-deviation

Run 2 · selection and loss

  • Also add 1 − probe score to the training loss
  • Flags --deviation-loss-weight 3 --deviation-every 5

Both runs continued from the 45k hybrid. Both raised the probe score, and both lowered the held-out deviation r:

346 cells, from hybrid 45knuc profile racc AUROCprobe scoredeviation rshift r
hybrid, 45k steps0.3750.657—0.1840.166
hybrid, 77.5k steps0.3890.680—0.0480.155
run 1, selection0.3880.6780.264 → 0.2920.0300.118
run 2, selection and loss0.3900.688→ 0.3300.009−0.031

Run 2, which trained directly on the probe, did worst: its shift r became negative. A training target that goes up while the independent eval goes down is overfit.

Cause · the probe was too narrow

The probe had 192 windows and only the 12 deepest cell types, mostly retina and brain. It ranked finished checkpoints in the correct order (0.122, 0.008, 0.001, against eval values of 0.184, 0.048, 0.027). But during training, the model raised the score on this narrow set without improvement on the broad eval panel.

Lesson

A proxy that ranks finished checkpoints correctly can still fail as a training target. Check that the proxy and the eval improve together during training. The fine-tune in section 11 uses this lesson: it selects on separate validation chromosomes and balances the tissue contexts.

10 · Adult cells only

70% of candidate loci stay differential in adult cells

The 841 calls in section 03 came from a panel with fetal cells and cell lines. We scored the same loci again in an adult-only cohort: 244 pseudobulk tracks in 16 tissue contexts, with no fetal samples and no cell lines. Each target cell is compared with other adult cells from the same tissue context (primary call) and with all adult cells (secondary call).

The candidate sites do not come only from fetal cells or cell lines. 70% of the loci keep an adult call, and the median shift (25 bp) is close to the value in the mixed panel (30 bp).

Limits

A tissue context is a screening group, not a molecular lineage. The tracks are pseudobulks, so the result gives no donor-to-donor variance. The 553 loci come from the earlier mixed screen, so this is not an unbiased adult atlas. An unbiased adult discovery run is not done.

11 · Heads-only fine-tune

The fine-tune is clean, but the gains are small

This run tested the option that section 09 left open: freeze the backbone and train only the per-cell heads. It also used the probe lessons. Training, validation and the challenge set use separate chromosomes and separate differential calls. Sampling is balanced across tissue contexts, so cardiac, PBMC and bone-marrow cells do not dominate.

Setup

  • Start: the 77.5k hybrid (model_346_hybrid_long.pt)
  • Trained: only the 244 adult rows of the nucleosome head weights
  • Frozen, and checked bit for bit: trunk, FiLM, embeddings, accessibility, count heads, all bias rows, and the 102 non-adult rows

Loss and selection

  • Loss: ordinary profile loss + profile loss at differential sites + λ × deviation loss
  • Pilot: λ = 0.25, 1 and 4, for 2,000 steps each. λ = 4 won
  • Final: 10,000 steps from the start weights. Step 9,750 was selected, and all 40 checkpoints passed the safety checks
  • Train on the training chromosomes, select on chr8 and chr20, challenge on chr1, chr3 and chr6

This section uses different metrics from sections 04 to 09. Deviation is measured against the other cells in the same tissue context, and values are pooled over all eligible cell × locus rows:

Validation: 2,050 context units, 29,468 cell × locus rows, 662 positives. Challenge: 553 loci, 4,993 context units, 44,082 rows, 983 positives. The ordinary nucleosome profile r changed by less than 0.0001, and the accessibility outputs did not change.

What worked

Unlike the probe runs, validation and challenge improve together. The fine-tune also changed nothing outside its scope: only the 244 adult head rows changed, and every other tensor is identical to the start checkpoint.

What did not

The gains are small: +0.003 deviation r on validation, +0.0015 on the challenge set, and no change in AUPRC. This is not a material improvement. The tuned checkpoint is experimental and does not replace the start checkpoint.

Caveats

The ordinary profile r in this evaluation (about 0.06 to 0.08) is per base and unsmoothed, so it does not compare with the smoothed 0.389. A first version of the evaluation weighted the effect and shift metrics by context, which was incorrect. We corrected it to pool over rows, and we kept the old outputs (pilot, pilot_ampfix, final) only as an audit record. The challenge evaluation ran once, after that correction, so it is a corrective re-evaluation, not an untouched test. The 553 challenge loci were selected because they looked differential, so the result does not estimate genome-wide performance.

Untested explanation: the fine-tune started from the 77.5k checkpoint, which had already lost most of its cell-type signal. If the frozen trunk no longer carries that signal, a head-only update cannot recover it. A fine-tune from the 45k checkpoint, or one that also unfreezes FiLM or the late trunk layers, would test this.

12 · Scope

What this shows, and what is open

Shown

  • The model limits the 0.37 profile r (47% of a 0.77 ceiling), not the data
  • About one third of positioning is cell-type-specific, and 70% of candidate loci keep an adult-only call
  • The deviation r and shift r metrics score the cell-type signal, not the backbone
  • A per-cell head raises shift r from about 0 to 0.17; FiLM cannot do this
  • Absolute metrics and deviation r move in opposite directions under longer training
  • A narrow probe overfits as a training target. A split-safe, tissue-balanced objective does not overfit, but on frozen heads its gains are small

Open

  • Fine-tune from the 45k checkpoint, which still has the cell-type signal
  • Stage 2: unfreeze FiLM or the late trunk layers (needs separate approval)
  • Unbiased adult discovery, and a test set that is not pre-selected
  • Donor-to-donor variance; tissue context against molecular lineage
  • One checkpoint that is good at both the backbone and the cell-type signal

Recommendation: use run/model_346_hybrid.pt (45k) for cell-type work and run/model_346_hybrid_long.pt (77.5k) for the absolute profile. Treat diffnuc_finetune/final_metricfix/final.pt as experimental.
